# Train of Thoughts

Open `/metro/` on the existing local server. A standalone Three.js experiment using the architectural monochrome treatment of Modular Habitat.

The scene stays square at every viewport size. On desktop the sidebar takes the remaining width; narrow screens stack it below. The orthographic camera sits farther from the city, with the same viewing angle and relative fog, so rotating cannot slice through nearby buildings. The connected city uses a 320-unit ring; five foreground rows extend toward the viewer, each with a 640-unit ring. All copies share geometry. The camera sits 225 units back with matching fog compensation, keeping foreground roofs away from the near clipping plane even at the maximum allowed orbit.

Rendering keeps all model detail and the same supersampling. Static matrices and outline buffers are reused; tight box culling skips city geometry outside the camera. Thought cards render in a separate, small scene only while visible. Passenger IK avoids solving the same arms twice. Whole-person bounds skip offscreen skin rendering. The sidebar-free diagnostics in `#stage[data-performance]` report actual FPS, CPU frame time and submitted geometry.

The cabin has its own root beside the passenger car roots. Its roof, side returns, connecting partition, glass and floor belong to that compartment. Closed side glass conceals the connecting door; selecting the cabin removes only its glass/roof/near wall. The previous compartment closes before the next opens. Hover contours use 1.75 CSS-pixel strokes with extra roof/side clearance with individual fade-in/fade-out. Redundant ground-floor window frames are removed. The storefront canopy now extends into the wall, with outlines only on its exposed front and side edges; its former rear-edge strokes at the facade joint are removed. Sam's shirt has a gently flattened chest plane and a softer lighting response; anatomy, skin weights and animation remain intact.

`qa-layout.html` checks square framing at desktop, portrait and mobile sizes. `check-aisle.mjs` checks a full 30-second walk and both turns against body capsules and every grab-pole/hand-loop segment. Hand loops sit beside the seats, outside the continuous aisle.

Track, street, connected building district and foreground receiving buildings all move at 1x, keeping bridges, beams and entrances aligned through the entire loop. Parallax uses 0.78x travel for the distant city and 0.62x for the skyline. Each layer is batched separately at startup.

Each includes real recesses, door hardware and thresholds; narrow bays use single portals or arches. Geometry joins the existing static city batches. `qa-entrances.html` shows all six together.
- Balcony guards close both sides back to the facade. Frameless glass, glass with a fine top cap and solid parapets use continuous base shoes, without intermediate rails or vertical cages. Glass has a subtle neutral tint and transparent surfaces; adjacent individual balconies have separate slabs.
- The train runs 11 units above its original elevation. The viaduct has flanged rails, fasteners, bolted girder panels, braced capitals, utility cabinets, pipe bundles and suspended cables.

- Four tree silhouettes use pointed foliage tiers and radial linework: spreading canopy, slender poplar, layered conifer and branching ornamental. Spherical foliage removed, including small rooftop plants. Ten pooled sidewalk pedestrians enter with randomized timing, looks, speeds and directions; each sidewalk has an independent arrival clock so neither can starve. The tree planters leave two walking lanes clear.

- Two opposing street lanes have independent, light traffic, capped at three cars per lane over the full route. Cars keep a 22-unit minimum center spacing, anticipate slower leaders at 28–38 units and enter with randomized, staggered delays. Rotating wheels and variable cruising speeds remain independent of the scenery batches.
- Up to fourteen bridge walkers reuse the Habitat meshes, captured gait and planted-foot IK. Only a subset of bridges is populated, with staggered arrivals, different starting positions and two passing lanes. Incoming scenery gets fresh crossings already underway outside the camera view, instead of starting everyone at hidden endpoints. Train scale remains 100%; bridges use 94% and sidewalks 89%, with shoe calibration at each final scale. Pause stops traffic and pedestrians together with the train.
- Train passengers and bridge walkers share ten coordinated grayscale looks: dark, gray and silver hair; light, mid-gray and charcoal outfits with contrasting trousers and shoes. The existing short-hair and ponytail silhouettes retain the same rigs and outlined cel materials; each walker keeps their appearance across arrivals.
- Static assemblies are merged by material; coincident outline segments are removed during batching. Architectural outlines use 0.85 CSS-pixel screen-space strips with alpha-to-coverage rather than one-framebuffer-pixel GL lines. Opaque surfaces use a constant two-unit depth offset with no slope-scaled bias, so shallow facade faces do not expose buried edges as they scroll. Speech-card outlines retain their separate animated materials and depth pass. Rendering samples at 1.5–2x display resolution.

Ten passengers and a motorman share Habitat's Sam/Maya geometry, cel shading, skinned outlines, articulated fingers and analytic IK. Seated passengers rest their hands on their thighs with uncrossed legs. Elbow IK follows each actual shoulder, keeping elbows by the ribs instead of flared outward; contact and the full motion cycle are checked. Standing grips remain attached to the poles; fingers wrap around the pole and the thumb opposes them, with skin clearance checked against the cylinder. The driver uses the seated pose with fixed wrist targets and opposing thumb/finger arcs around two upright controls. One passenger traverses the central aisle with Habitat's CMU walking cycle, planted-foot solver and spring-driven turns. This is contact-constrained animation, not a full rigid-body or learned motion-matching simulation. Asset and motion attribution: `../habitat/assets/people/CREDITS.md`. ## Performance and video capture

Animations keep their original detail, frame rate and solver. Outside a conservative whole-body camera volume, only clocks, contacts and motion springs advance; the exact pose is solved before re-entry. Shoe calibration is cached per rig and warmed during loading. Train picking excludes skinned meshes and mouse movement is resolved at most once per rendered frame. Speech textures/shaders are prepared during loading. `check-performance.mjs` compares resumed and continuously animated rigs after four seconds offscreen (zero matrix error), including walkers and cached shoe contacts. Run with the same node import-map loader as the other checks.

On this machine, sampled interior CPU frame cost decreased from 16.65 ms to 9.82 ms, at the same 1291-square viewport and supersampling. This is a local sample, not a frame-time guarantee on other devices. Diagnostics include p95/max frame intervals and time per animation subsystem.

Opening preparation also uploads hidden geometry and pooled character skin palettes behind the loading cover. Identical skeleton palettes are shared within each character, reducing ten palette updates/uploads to one without changing any vertex or coupling different characters. `check-rig-performance.mjs` verifies exact skinned vertex equality; `check-performance.mjs` verifies pose continuity when entering view. Picking uses precomputed train bounds and the draw loop reuses its resident list.

`data-transition-performance` on the stage reports the first 2.2 seconds after focusing a carriage. A local 1291-square opening sample improved from 13.00 to 10.04 ms CPU per frame, with p95 frame intervals from 33.3 to 19.1 ms (the procedural street varies between runs). Resolution, antialiasing, geometry, outlines and animation rate are unchanged. `check-pause-resume.mjs` verifies both pedestrian models and directions through repeated pauses, including forward motion relative to the scrolling pavement. In the train's reference frame a person walking against the scenery's scroll can still move backwards on screen; the pavement-relative walk remains forward.

Run `powershell -File metro/start-video.ps1` alongside the local preview. It starts `video-server.py` on loopback port 4174 and uses installed FFmpeg (PATH or Shutter Encoder). The service accepts mutations only from the local preview origins. No publishing or remote upload is involved.

Click Record to capture the live camera, poses, city, vehicles, panels and speech cards. Pause ends the take. Captured transforms are interpolated into 60 frames/second and re-rendered from the original geometry at 2048 x 2048, then encoded as H.264/yuv420p MP4 with fast-start. Encoding happens after capture so 2K rendering does not compete with live navigation. Only the scene is exported; sidebar, cursor and controls are excluded. Cancel export restores the captured scene. The scene remains paused after export. Download is also available as a persistent link. The local master is saved in `metro/exports/`.

The capture buffer automatically finalizes at 384 MiB; switching away from the tab also ends the take. This prevents unbounded memory use. X may recompress or restrict high-resolution uploads depending on account and upload surface.

Close-up interaction uses bone-following capsules for passenger body picking; face cards keep their geometry-based picking. This avoids CPU skinning the full detailed mesh on each pointer movement (local benchmark: 64.46 ms per detailed query versus 0.0092 ms for the capsules). These invisible interaction volumes do not replace any rendered geometry. Verify head, torso, legs, compartment filtering and missed rays with `check-person-picking.mjs`.

Export camera states use double-precision position, focus, zoom and projection, independently of the float32 skeletal stream. The view is reconstructed from the interpolated focus instead of independently interpolating translation and rotation. The camera is exempt from the 8-unit scenery-recycling discontinuity rule. Fixed train/floor/bridge transforms are not rewritten during replay; only their moving panels, wheels, occupants and scrolling parents are captured. `check-recorded-camera.mjs` checks stationary views and focus lock across a camera move exceeding that threshold. This change affects new recordings; an already encoded MP4 is unchanged.
